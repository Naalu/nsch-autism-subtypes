# Validation checklist

Clustering always produces groups, so a subtyping study has to show that its groups are more than an artifact of one sample and one algorithm. Agelink van Rentergem, Deserno and Geurts (2021) reviewed 156 autism subtyping studies from 2001 to 2020 and distilled the ways of showing that into a seven-item checklist, SUVAC (SUbtyping VAlidation Checklist). The table below says how this project handles each item and what happened. The right-hand column is the share of the 156 reviewed studies that used each strategy; no study used all seven, and most used one or two.

The methods named in the table (ARI, bootstrap Jaccard, entropy) are explained in plain language in `docs/background.md` section 4. Numbers below are from the reference-backend runs of October 19, 2026 (`results/`, `results_k4/`, `results_k5/`) and will be re-verified with the from-scratch code.

| SUVAC item | What it asks | This project | Outcome at K=4 | Share of reviewed studies |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cross-method replication | Is more than one subtyping method applied? | Yes. Latent class model and k-means on the same children, cut at the same K, compared with the adjusted Rand index. Pass line ARI ≥ 0.65. `scripts/04_crosscheck.py`. | **Fails**, ARI 0.57. See "Decisions" for why, and the method table in `reports/figures/results/method_comparison.csv`. | 13% |
| Subtype separation | Is distinctiveness or assignment certainty reported? | Yes. Relative entropy and smallest class share for every K (`results/lca_bic.csv`), class probabilities carried into the weighted shares, and cluster-wise bootstrap Jaccard (`scripts/06_bootstrap.py`). | Entropy 0.72. All four classes bootstrap-stable (Jaccard 0.91 to 0.93). | 38% |
| Independent replication | Is the analysis repeated in a second sample? | Yes, twice. Fit on 2016 to 2021, refit on 2022 to 2024, ARI on the 2022 to 2024 children (`scripts/05_replicate.py`). Then the 2025 file, released after the design was fixed, labeled by the frozen 2016 to 2024 model and by a fresh refit (`scripts/08_forward_2025.py`). | Passes: 0.72 across years, 0.79 on 2025. 2025 shares within two to three points of pooled. | 9% |

Count plus class beats count alone (BIC 14,450 vs 14,873); class alone does not (14,999). | 88% |

Two checks beyond SUVAC were added after the first results and are reported with the same pass lines: refits within age bands 6 to 11 and 12 to 17 against the pooled model (`scripts/09_age_bands.py`; ARI 0.70 and 0.78, so the axes are not an age artifact), and robustness to survey-weighted fitting, complete-case exclusion and random seed (`scripts/11_robustness.py`; weighted-fit ARI 0.79, dropped children differ little from kept, seed-to-seed ARI 0.99).

## Pass lines and where they come from

- **ARI ≥ 0.65.** Steinley (2004) gives rough bands for the adjusted Rand index: 0.90 and up excellent, 0.80 to 0.90 good, 0.65 to 0.80 moderate, below 0.65 poor. We use the floor of "moderate" because the comparisons are between different children (held-out years) or very different methods, where near-perfect agreement is not expected. In a simulation with three equal groups, moving about one child in eight to a wrong group gives an ARI near 0.65.
- **Mean bootstrap Jaccard ≥ 0.75** for a stable class, ≤ 0.5 for a dissolved one, from Hennig (2007). 200 resamples, twice the default in `fpc::clusterboot`.
- **Number of classes by BIC**, following Nylund, Asparouhov and Muthén (2007), with the rule below for the case where BIC does not turn up.

## Decisions taken after the first results (October 19, 2026)

These are changes from the proposal. They were made after seeing the sweep over K, and the paper says so.

**1. Choice of K.** BIC fell at every K from 1 to 8 and would have chosen whatever upper limit we set. The drops shrink from 8,224 (K=1 to 2) to 6 (K=7 to 8), the column-shuffled baseline rises throughout, and classes added past K=5 hold under 3% of children each. The rule adopted: the largest K at which every class is bootstrap-stable (mean Jaccard ≥ 0.75) and the held-out-years replication passes (ARI ≥ 0.65). That is K=4. K=5 is reported as a sensitivity analysis: it adds a small (8%) severe class with intellectual disability and medical conditions whose existence replicates but whose membership does not (Jaccard 0.62, replication ARI 0.65 on the line, weighted-fit ARI 0.58). Because the 2025 forward test was in the same table we chose from, it is confirmatory rather than fully independent. Full table: `reports/tables/checks_by_k.csv`.

**2. Cross-check method.** The proposal named Jaccard distance with average linkage. On this data it places nearly every child in one cluster at any K (ARI 0.00 against the classes), because the 18-item binary matrix has no cluster geometry: Jaccard distance takes only 97 distinct values across 39.5 million pairs, 26% of pairs sit at the maximum distance, and the 517 children with no conditions are a single point. Complete linkage, Ward, inverse-prevalence-weighted Jaccard and spectral clustering on a Jaccard graph all collapse the same way. k-means on the flags is the reported second method (ARI 0.57, below the line); it agrees with the latent classes on the two axes and disagrees about where to cut them, which is what a dimensional structure predicts. The hierarchical implementation stays in the project because it is in the proposal and because reproducing the degenerate result is how we know it matches SciPy.

**3. The dimensional reading.** Because every method that found structure found two axes, we fit a two-dimensional latent trait model (binary factor analysis, `src/nsch_subtypes/latent_trait.py`, `scripts/12_dimensions.py`) to the same data. It has the lowest BIC of any model fitted (105,298 with 53 parameters, against 106,428 for K=4 and 105,656 for K=8 with 151), its loadings split the conditions cleanly into a developmental-language axis and a psychiatric axis, and a two-cut rule on its scores reproduces the K=4 classes at ARI 0.79. Data simulated from that continuum, with no classes in it, give a latent class BIC curve that keeps falling through K=6 with entropy 0.77 to 0.70, which is the real curve. We therefore describe the result as two dimensions whose four combinations are stable, replicable regions, not as four subtypes. This follows Bauer and Curran (2004) on what latent class analysis does to continuous data.

## Related results to compare against

Unsupervised subtyping has no benchmark with ground truth, so "state of the art" means the published subtype solutions closest to ours and the strongest sensible methods on our own data.

| Study | Sample | Inputs | Method | Classes | Validation used |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Doshi-Velez, Ge & Kohane 2014 | ~4,900 autistic patients, one hospital's records; 496 from another region | Diagnosis-code time series, ages 0 to 15 | Hierarchical clustering | 3 small distinct groups (seizure, multisystem, psychiatric) + 1 large unresolved | Replication sample |
| Lingren et al. 2016 | 20,658 autistic patients, children's hospital records | Comorbidity codes | Clustering (secondary analysis) | Psychiatric, developmental, seizure | None reported |
| Vargason, Frye, McGuinness & Hahn 2019 | 3,278 autistic children, private insurance claims | Co-occurring condition patterns, first 5 years | k-means | 3: high rates of everything (23.7%), developmental delay (26.5%), low rates (49.8%) | None reported |
| Montgomery et al. 2023, Australian Autism Biobank | 754 of 1,151 autistic children, ages 2 to 17 | 37 indicators: autism traits, cognition, psychiatric, medical | Latent profile analysis | 4, from fewer support needs (29%) to high medical and psychiatric comorbidity (26%) | Authors call for replication |
| Litman et al. 2025, *Nature Genetics* | >5,000 autistic children aged 4 to 18 in SPARK, with genetics | Binary, categorical and continuous traits incl. co-occurring conditions | Generative mixture model | 4: social/behavioral challenges (37%), mixed ASD with developmental delay (19%), moderate challenges (34%), broadly affected (10%) | Independent cohort |
| This project | 9,710 autistic children aged 6 to 17, NSCH 2016 to 2024; 1,660 in 2025 | 18 parent-reported current conditions | Latent class model (EM), from scratch, against a latent trait model | 4 at the chosen rule: few other conditions 34%, developmental-language 33%, psychiatric 18%, both 16%; a severe 8% class at K=5 | Cross-method (fails), two independent replications, parallel, bootstrap, age bands, external (severity), continuum comparison |

Litman's four SPARK classes (37 / 19 / 34 / 10) and ours (18 psychiatric / 33 developmental / 34 few / 16 both, with an 8% severe class at K=5) are not a one-to-one match, but a nationally representative survey with 18 yes/no items landing on the same broad shape is the closest comparison available. The "broadly affected" class that Vargason (24%) and Litman (10%) report corresponds to our K=5 fifth class, and our bootstrap says its membership is not stable in parent-reported data.

On our own data the method comparisons are: the from-scratch code against StepMix and SciPy (correctness), k-means and six other methods against the latent classes (second method), the column-shuffled baseline (no structure), the condition count against the classes on severity (the planned null), and the two-dimensional latent trait model against every K (types or dimensions).
